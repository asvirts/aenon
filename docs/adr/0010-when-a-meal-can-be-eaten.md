# When a meal can be eaten

Food refills current Hitpoints, the pool bounded by the Hitpoints skill, and eating leaves Adventurer level where it is. The Adventurer eats from what they carry, in a settlement or in the wilderness. Any rarity eats at the same moment. The hearth is where the meal is made. While no fight is underway and the pool is short, the next meal may follow at once. A shortfall spends the meal, and the pool stops at the skill. A full pool keeps the meal. A fight keeps it from the first swing until the hostile falls or the Adventurer wakes in the last settlement. Before that first swing, the Adventurer may eat. A set scene that is playing keeps the meal. A scripture reading leaves it available, and walking, a gather, and a hunt continue. Raw fish, raw meat, and herbs restore nothing. Making the meal still pays the smaller Adventurer grant inside the place's span, recorded in `docs/adr/0009-combat-skills-are-the-adventurer-level.md`.

Eating inside the melee would have put a second action beside the one swing. The heal on a legendary piece is the recovery inside that melee. A wait between meals would have carried a combat delay into peacetime. Spending a meal on a full pool would have destroyed food for no refill. Holding the meal until the whole restore fit would have left a shortfall unfilled. A legendary meal in the melee, or raw meat that restored the pool, would have made food a potion. Eating only at a hearth would have taken the meal off the road, where the pool falls. Stopping a gather to eat would have made eating a second station.

## Considered options

- Eating during a fight.
- A legendary meal eaten during a fight, and a lesser meal kept for the road.
- A wait between meals.
- Keeping the meal unless the whole restore fits in the pool.
- Spending the meal when the pool is already full.
- Raw meat or herbs restoring current Hitpoints.
- Eating only at a hearth.
- Eating stops the gather.
- A playing set scene allowing the meal.
- A scripture reading keeping the meal back.
