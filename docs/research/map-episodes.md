# Which episodes belong on this map

The Season is the single playable stretch of about 27–29 AD, with John still baptizing at Aenon and the region's episodes present together. Play does not continue into the arrest or the cross. Luke 3:1 dates the start of John's ministry to the fifteenth year of Tiberius. This note does not reopen that calculation. It uses the Season as the project has already fixed it.

The sheet prints a question mark on Aenon. For this project the pin is fixed: Aenon near Salim, on the Jordan, just south of Scythopolis. The Samaria / Wadi Farah tradition is a real debate, and it is not the site used here.

References are by book, chapter, and verse. Josephus is cited by work and section. Eusebius's *Onomasticon* is cited by the entry name. No Bible prose is copied.

## How a placement is marked

**The text places this here in this Season.** The Gospels locate the episode at this place while John is still free. The backbone is John 1–4. John 3:24 says outright that John had not yet been put in prison. John 4 never cancels that sentence. Mark 1:14 and Matthew 4:12 are the line after it: when John is arrested, Jesus withdraws into Galilee and, in Matthew 4:13, leaves Nazareth to live in Capernaum. Luke 3:19–20 mentions the imprisonment before the baptism. That is Luke's preview. Luke 3:21 still tells the baptism after it, so the preview does not move the baptism out of the Season.

**Compressed onto this Season.** A famous episode the Gospels set after John's imprisonment, and still before the arrest and the cross. Many of these still fall inside the years 27–29. They do not fall in the same week as Aenon. The map lays them on one Season anyway, so the region can be walked in a single stretch. Mark 6:14–29 tells John's death, as a flashback, before the feeding of the five thousand. Episodes from that feeding onward are compression on Mark's clock even when a year inside 27–29 could still hold them.

**Memory in the landscape.** An older episode the place still carries: Judges, Samuel, Kings, and the childhood narratives from before 27. It is not happening this week.

**Outside the Season.** The arrest, the cross, the resurrection, and later history such as the war of 66–70. These are named so they are not staged here. They are not compressed onto the Season.

Hometown lines are not scenes. John 1:44 can place a disciple's town in the Season without a story being played in that town.

## Decision

While John is still baptizing, the Gospels place only a few scenes on this sheet. John is at Aenon near Salim (John 3:23–36). The wedding at Cana is the first sign (John 2:1–11). Jesus, his mother, his brothers, and his disciples stay briefly at Capernaum (John 2:12). Back in Cana, he heals a royal official's son who is sick in Capernaum (John 4:46–54). He comes from Nazareth to be baptized (Mark 1:9), and Philip, Andrew, and Peter are from Bethsaida (John 1:44). Those are origins. The scenes are not played inside those towns. The baptism itself is in the Jordan, and John 1:28 puts that earlier work at Bethany beyond the Jordan, off this sheet.

Everything else famous here is compression or memory. The large compressions are Capernaum as his home, the lake (the calls, the storm, both feedings, the walk on the water, the landing at Gennesaret), the rejection at Nazareth, the widow's son at Nain, and the demoniac in the country of the Gadarenes or the Gerasenes. Putting the Transfiguration on Tabor is compression onto a mountain the Gospels never name. Saul on Gilboa and at Beth-shan, Deborah on Tabor and at the Kishon, Gideon at Moreh, the town of Jezreel, Megiddo under the label Legio, and Jonah's Gath-hepher are memory. Sepphoris, Pella, Hippus, Jotapata, and Gamla have no Gospel scene in the Season. Tiberias is already standing, and Jesus is never said to enter it. The sieges of 67 and the flight to Pella are later, and they stay off this Season.

The sheet also prints **Esdraelon** on the valley floor. That is the Greek name of Jezreel, and it is filed under the Jezreel below. It is not an extra settlement.

## Settlements

### Aenon

The only biblical occurrence is John 3:23. John was baptizing at Aenon near Salim, because there was much water there, and people were coming to be baptized. John 3:25–36, the dispute about purification and John's testimony about the bridegroom, is told while he is at that place. John 3:26 looks back to Jesus as the one who had been with John "across the Jordan," which is the earlier site, not a second name for Aenon.

The verse says "much water." It does not say the word Jordan. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Aenon, says the place was still pointed out in his day, eight miles south of Scythopolis, near Salim and the Jordan. That is the pin: west of the Jordan, south of Scythopolis, with Salim just north of it. The printed question mark records the debate. The other candidate is the springs of Wadi Farah, near a Salim east of Shechem. This map does not use that site.

**The text places this here in this Season.** John's baptizing at Aenon, and the conversation in John 3:25–36. John 3:24: he had not yet been imprisoned.

Jesus is nearby in the narrative and not at this pin. John 3:22 has Jesus baptizing in the Judean countryside. That countryside is off this sheet. John 4:1–3, he leaves Judea for Galilee. He is not described as standing in the water at Aenon.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing later in the ministry is moved here.

**Memory in the landscape.** None. The Hebrew Bible does not name Aenon.

**Outside the Season.** John 10:40–42 has Jesus return to the place where John was baptizing at first, across the Jordan. That is late, close to the final journey, and it names the first site. It is not laid on this pin.

### Salim

Salim appears once, as the locator for Aenon (John 3:23). No scene is told inside the village.

Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Salem, distinguishes three uses of the name. One is the Salem of the Shechemites (Genesis 33:18, and the word there can be read as a place or as "safe"). One is a village shown near Jerusalem. One is Salumias, in the plain, at the eighth mile toward Scythopolis. The same entry reports Josephus's identification of Melchizedek's Salem (Genesis 14:18) with the city later called Jerusalem. The Salim on this sheet is Salumias, the village paired with Aenon. It is not Jerusalem, and it is not Shechem.

**The text places this here in this Season.** The name only, as the town Aenon is near. John is baptizing at Aenon, not in a narrated scene at Salim.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** Nothing that belongs to this Salim. Genesis 14:18 and Genesis 33:18 belong to other Salems.

### Scythopolis

The Gospels never use the name. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Beth-shan, identifies Beth-shan with Scythopolis. Josephus calls Scythopolis the largest city of the Decapolis, in the neighborhood of Tiberias (*Jewish War* 3.446). It is the Decapolis city on the west side of the Jordan. Aenon is measured south from it.

**The text places this here in this Season.** No Gospel episode. The city is standing. Jesus is never said to enter it. Crowds from the Decapolis (Matthew 4:25), the healed man's preaching in the Decapolis (Mark 5:20), and the route through the Decapolis (Mark 7:31) do not name Scythopolis.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing is moved into the city.

**Memory in the landscape.** Beth-shan.

- Joshua 17:11 and Judges 1:27: Manasseh did not drive out the inhabitants of Beth-shan.
- 1 Kings 4:12: Beth-shan in one of Solomon's districts, with Taanach and Megiddo.
- 1 Samuel 31:8–13 and 1 Chronicles 10:8–12: the Philistines fasten Saul's body to the wall of Beth-shan. The men of Jabesh-gilead take it down and bury it. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Jabesh-gilead, puts that town beyond the Jordan, six miles from Pella on the road to Gerasa. Jabesh-gilead is not a label on this sheet. The body is at Beth-shan. The rescuers come from east of the river.

**Outside the Season.** At the start of the war in 66, Josephus tells a massacre of the Jewish inhabitants of Scythopolis (*Jewish War* 2.466; *Life* 26). That stays off this Season.

### Pella

Pella is not in the Bible. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Decapolis, lists Hippos, Pella, and Gadara among those cities, east of the river. The map's dot matches that: east of the Jordan, south of Gadara, opposite the Aenon reach.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The Gospels are silent.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** No episode is sited in the city. The Jabesh-gilead of 1 Samuel 31:11–13 is in the country Eusebius measures from Pella. That episode is told under Scythopolis, because the wall is Beth-shan.

**Outside the Season.** Eusebius, *Church History* 3.5.3, says the church in Jerusalem was warned before the war to leave the city and dwell in a town of Perea called Pella. That is about 66–70. It is not in the Gospels, and Josephus never mentions it. It is not compressed onto this Season. The report itself is fourth-century, and readers who notice Josephus's silence are right to treat it as a later tradition rather than a Season scene.

### Gadara

Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Gadara, places the city beyond the Jordan, opposite Scythopolis and Tiberias, eastward in the mountains, with hot baths at its foot. Josephus, *Life* 349, puts Gadara sixty furlongs from Tiberias, and Hippos thirty. After Herod's death, Gadara and Hippos were cut off from the kingdom and added to Syria (*Jewish War* 2.97). In this Season they are Decapolis cities, not towns of Antipas's Galilee. Josephus, *Life* 42, has villages of Gadara and of Hippos on the border of Tiberias and of the region of Scythopolis. The city's territory could touch the lake country even though the city itself sits back in the hills. The map's dot is that hill city, southeast of the lake.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The demoniac is later.

**Compressed onto this Season.** The demoniac and the pigs, during the Galilean ministry, after John's imprisonment.

- Matthew 8:28–34 sets it in the country of the Gadarenes.
- Mark 5:1–20 and Luke 8:26–39 set it in the country of the Gerasenes.

The Greek copies disagree. Matthew's usual reading is Gadarenes, with Gerasenes and Gergesenes in other copies. Mark and Luke's usual reading is Gerasenes, with Gadarenes and Gergesenes in other copies. Gerasa (Jerash) is far from the lake, a poor fit for a herd running into the sea. Gadara's territory, on Josephus's evidence, reached toward Tiberias, so "the country of the Gadarenes" can mean the shore without meaning the hilltop city. A third candidate, Gergesa, is the steep east shore (often pointed out at Kursi). Kursi is not a label on this sheet. The map labels Gadara, so the episode is carried with Gadara, and the site stays debated. It is not moved onto Hippus. Hippus has a steep ridge opposite Tiberias, and the text does not name it.

Afterward the man proclaims in the Decapolis (Mark 5:20). That region is wider than Gadara.

**Memory in the landscape.** None in the Bible.

**Outside the Season.** The burning of Gadara's villages in the war (*Life* 42) stays off the Season.

### Nazareth

Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Nazareth, still knows a village of that name in Galilee, fifteen miles east of Legio, near Mount Tabor. The map puts it in the hills south of Sepphoris and north of the Jezreel, which agrees with that. The Hebrew Bible does not name Nazareth. Matthew 2:23 says the move there fulfilled what was spoken by the prophets, "he shall be called a Nazarene," but it does not quote a single verse that contains the name. Josephus never mentions Nazareth.

**The text places this here in this Season.** An origin, not a scene inside the town. Jesus comes from Nazareth in Galilee to be baptized (Mark 1:9; Matthew 3:13 says from Galilee to the Jordan). In the early days at the Jordan, Philip calls him Jesus of Nazareth, and Nathanael answers out of the town's reputation (John 1:45–46). That conversation is where John is baptizing, not in Nazareth.

**Compressed onto this Season.** The rejection, after John is arrested.

- Matthew 4:12–13: when Jesus hears that John has been arrested, he withdraws into Galilee, leaves Nazareth, and lives in Capernaum.
- Luke 4:16–30: in the synagogue at Nazareth, on the Sabbath, he reads from Isaiah and they try to throw him off the brow of the hill on which the town was built. Luke sets this at the opening of his Galilean preaching.
- Mark 6:1–6 and Matthew 13:54–58 tell a rejection later, after a body of miracles, and say he could do little there because of their unbelief.

Whether Luke 4 and Mark 6 are one visit or two is debated. Neither visit is while John is still at Aenon. The hill in Luke 4:29 is "the brow of the hill on which their town was built." The text does not name a particular cliff.

**Memory in the landscape.** Before 27.

- The annunciation (Luke 1:26–38).
- Joseph goes up from Nazareth to Bethlehem (Luke 2:4).
- The family returns to Nazareth after the birth (Luke 2:39) and again after the boy is found in the temple (Luke 2:51–52).
- After Egypt, they settle in Nazareth (Matthew 2:19–23).

**Outside the Season.** Nothing further is required here. The arrest and the cross are not in this town.

### Cana

The Gospel name is Cana of Galilee (John 2:1; 4:46; 21:2). John 2:12, they go *down* from Cana to Capernaum, so the narrative puts Cana in the hills above the lake. The map draws it just south of Jotapata and north of Sepphoris. That is the northern Galilean village, above the plain Josephus calls Asochis (*Life* 207), not the later pilgrimage village east of Nazareth (Kafr Kanna). Which ruin is the Gospel village is still debated. The sheet has chosen the northern dot.

Josephus, *Life* 86, was staying in a village of Galilee named Cana during the war of 66–67. That shows a Cana in Galilee in the first century. It does not by itself pick the ruin. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Kana, mixes the wedding with Kanah of Asher near Great Sidon (Joshua 19:28). His entry does not cleanly fix the site. Kanah of Asher is off this sheet, toward the northwest.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Both signs, on John's clock.

- John 2:1–11: the wedding, the water become wine, called the first sign. It follows the calling of the first disciples at the Jordan (John 1:35–51) and comes before the short stay at Capernaum and the Passover in Jerusalem (John 2:12–13). His mother is there. This is before Aenon in the narrative, and before any imprisonment.
- John 4:46–54: he comes again to Cana, and a royal official whose son is sick in Capernaum asks him to come down. The son is healed at a distance. John 4:54 calls this the second sign after he had come out of Judea into Galilee. The sequence between Aenon and this sign runs through Samaria (John 4:4–42), which is off this sheet.

John's Gospel does not say John has been imprisoned before this second sign. Mark 1:14 and Matthew 4:12 put a withdrawal into Galilee after the arrest. Those may or may not be the same journey as John 4. This note leaves the second sign in the Season, because John 3:24 has not been cancelled, and it leaves the taking up of residence in Capernaum on the other side of the line. See Capernaum.

The official in John 4 and the centurion in Matthew 8:5–13 and Luke 7:1–10 are different narratives: a royal official and his son, from Cana, on John's early clock; a centurion and his servant, in Capernaum, during the later Galilean ministry. They are not collapsed here.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing further in the town.

**Memory in the landscape.** None.

**Outside the Season.** John 21:2 names Nathanael of Cana in Galilee among the disciples at the lake after the resurrection. The breakfast in John 21 is after the cross. It is not placed in this Season. The hometown fact is the same Cana.

### Sepphoris

Sepphoris is not in the Bible. Josephus is the source for the city.

- *Jewish War* 2.56: after Herod's death (4 BC), Judas son of Hezekiah breaks open the royal armory at Sepphoris.
- *Jewish War* 2.68: Varus's general Caius takes Sepphoris, burns it, and enslaves its inhabitants.
- *Antiquities* 18.27: Herod the tetrarch (Antipas) walls Sepphoris, which Josephus calls the security of all Galilee, and makes it the metropolis.
- *Antiquities* 18.36: he afterward builds Tiberias on the lake.

So in this Season Sepphoris is the rebuilt older capital, and Tiberias is the new one. The Gospels never send Jesus there. Any story in which he works as a builder in Sepphoris is a modern inference. It is not in the text, and it is not used here.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The biblical text is silent.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** The armory and the burning in 4 BC (*Jewish War* 2.56, 2.68), a generation before the Season.

**Outside the Season.** Sepphoris's loyalty to Rome in 66–67, when the city admitted a Roman garrison sent by Cestius Gallus (*Life* 347), is later than this Season and is not laid on it.

### Gath-hepher

Joshua 19:13 puts Gath-hepher (Gittah-hepher) on the border of Zebulun. 2 Kings 14:25 says Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet, was from Gath-hepher. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Gath-hepher, repeats the Zebulun allotment, and under the Jonah entry calls it the prophet's home. He does not give a mileage. The map puts the dot between Sepphoris and Nazareth, which is the usual identification (the village of Mashhad). That identification is traditional. The verses do not name the ruin.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. No Gospel scene happens here.

**Compressed onto this Season.** The sign-of-Jonah sayings (Matthew 12:39–41; 16:4; Luke 11:29–32) belong to the later ministry, and they are not spoken at Gath-hepher. They are not moved onto this dot. Jonah's own story is Nineveh, off this sheet.

**Memory in the landscape.** The town as Jonah's home (2 Kings 14:25), inside Zebulun (Joshua 19:13).

### Nain

Luke 7:11–17 is the only biblical mention. Jesus comes to a town called Nain, and at the gate he raises the son of a widow. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Endor, already joins this episode to Nain and places Endor near it, near Scythopolis. The village on the north slope of Moreh (Nein) is the site, and it matches the map: south of Nazareth, on Moreh, above the Jezreel. There is little real debate about that village.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. Luke 7:11 follows the centurion at Capernaum (Luke 7:1–10). Luke 7:18–23, immediately after Nain, has John's disciples report to John, and Matthew 11:2 says John heard about these works in prison. Nain is after the imprisonment.

**Compressed onto this Season.** The widow's son (Luke 7:11–17).

**Memory in the landscape.** Not in Nain itself. Two neighboring stories are easy to merge and do not belong to this label. Shunem, on the south side of Moreh, is where Elisha raises the Shunammite's son (2 Kings 4:8–37) and where the Philistines camp opposite Saul on Gilboa (1 Samuel 28:4). Endor, which Eusebius puts near Nain, is where Saul consults the medium (1 Samuel 28:7–25). Neither village is a label on this sheet. Nain's episode is the widow in Luke 7, not Elisha and not the medium.

### Legio

The Gospels and Josephus do not use the name Legio. Eusebius, early in the fourth century, measures other places from Legio: Nazareth fifteen miles east of it, Taanach three or four miles from it, and the village Esdraela in the plain between Scythopolis and Legio (*Onomasticon*, under Nazareth, Taanach, and Jezreel). The biblical name of this crossroads is Megiddo. The label on the sheet is the later name. The map puts it at the west end of the Jezreel, where the Kishon runs, which is Megiddo's place.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** The Megiddo episodes, under this later name.

- Joshua 12:21: the king of Megiddo is among the defeated kings.
- Joshua 17:11 and Judges 1:27: Manasseh did not drive out the inhabitants of Megiddo.
- Judges 5:19: the kings of Canaan fought at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo. The same campaign is the Kishon and Mount Tabor (Judges 4).
- 1 Kings 4:12: Solomon's officer over Taanach, Megiddo, and Beth-shan.
- 1 Kings 9:15: Solomon's levy builds Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer.
- 2 Kings 9:27: Ahaziah of Judah dies at Megiddo, in Jehu's coup.
- 2 Kings 23:29–30 and 2 Chronicles 35:20–24: Pharaoh Neco kills Josiah at Megiddo.
- Zechariah 12:11: mourning in the plain of Megiddo.

**Outside the Season.** Revelation 16:16 gathers the kings to a place called Armageddon, the hill of Megiddo. That is an apocalyptic name, not an event of 27–29, and it is not staged in this Season.

### Jotapata

Jotapata is not in the Bible. Josephus, *Jewish War* 3.158, describes it as a town built on a precipice, approachable on the north, which he had walled when he fortified it. The siege and his capture are *Jewish War* 3.132–408, in 67. The map puts it in the hills north of Cana, which matches his description of a town hidden among the ridges of lower Galilee.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The Gospels are silent. No source narrates a scene here in 27–29. The town existed. Josephus treated it as a place worth fortifying a generation later. That is not an episode of the Season.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** None in the Bible.

**Outside the Season.** The siege of 67 is the famous episode, and it is not compressed onto this Season.

### Tiberias

Antipas built Tiberias on the lake of Gennesaret and named it for Tiberius (*Antiquities* 18.36–38). He compelled people to settle there. Josephus says many tombs were removed to make room, so that living there conflicted with the ancestral rule about corpse-impurity. The city is standing before the Gospel scenes that use the name. Josephus, *Life* 349, puts it on the lake, thirty furlongs from Hippos, sixty from Gadara, and a hundred and twenty from Scythopolis. The map's dot, on the west shore south of Magdala, matches that.

**The text places this here in this Season.** No scene inside the city. Jesus is never said to enter Tiberias.

**Compressed onto this Season.** The name, and boats from the city, at the feeding of the five thousand.

- John 6:1 calls the lake the Sea of Galilee, that is, the Sea of Tiberias.
- John 6:23: boats from Tiberias come near the place where the crowd had eaten. The meal is on the shore, not in the city. See the Sea of Galilee for the feeding itself, and for why it is compression.

**Memory in the landscape.** The founding, in Antipas's reign, before these Gospel scenes (*Antiquities* 18.36–38). Josephus does not give a year in that paragraph. The city is already named by John 6, so it is standing during the ministry.

**Outside the Season.** John 21:1, the Sea of Tiberias after the resurrection, is off this Season. The war around the city in 67 is off this Season.

### Magdala

"Mary called Magdalene" travels with Jesus in the Galilean ministry, and seven demons had gone out of her (Luke 8:2). The exorcism is not narrated, and it is not given a town. The usual reading of the name is "of Magdala." A minority reading takes *magdala* as a title ("tower") rather than a town. This note follows the place-name, and records the alternative.

The landing after the feeding of the four thousand is the region of Magadan (Matthew 15:39; some copies read Magdala) and the district of Dalmanutha (Mark 8:10). Neither name is explained. They are usually taken as the same west-shore landing, and that landing is usually identified with Magdala. The identification is debated.

Josephus never uses the name Magdala. He knows Taricheae, a city on the lake in lower Galilee, and he tells the fighting there after Jotapata (*Jewish War* 3.462ff). *Taricheae* means fish-salting. Equating that city with the Gospel Magdala is the usual geographical inference, and it is an inference. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Magdala, lists a Magdala in the tribe of Judah. That is a different place, off this sheet. It is not the west-shore town.

The map puts Magdala on the west shore between Gennesaret and Tiberias. That is the position the inference requires.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing while John is still at Aenon.

**Compressed onto this Season.**

- Mary's presence with the traveling company (Luke 8:1–3), which Luke places after Nain and after the message from John in prison. Her deliverance is background, not a scene staged in the town.
- The landing at Magadan / Dalmanutha after the four thousand (Matthew 15:39; Mark 8:10), late in the Galilean ministry.

**Memory in the landscape.** None in the Bible under this name.

**Outside the Season.** Mark 16:9, in the longer ending, mentions the seven demons in a resurrection scene. That ending is textually disputed, and the scene is after the cross. The fighting at Taricheae in 67 is outside the Season.

### Capernaum

Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Capernaum, places a village by the lake of Gennesaret, in "Galilee of the nations," on the border of Zebulun and Naphtali. That is him applying Matthew 4:13–15. The map's dot is the northwest shore. Josephus, *Jewish War* 3.519, gives the name Capernaum to the fountain that waters the plain of Gennesaret. The Gospels and Eusebius give it to the village. Whether his fountain is the spring at the village or the springs just south of it is debated. The episode list below belongs to the village the Gospels name.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Two early touches, before the imprisonment is announced.

- John 2:12: after the wedding at Cana, Jesus goes down to Capernaum with his mother, his brothers, and his disciples, and they stay not many days. Then John 2:13, the Passover in Jerusalem, which is off this sheet. This stay is before Aenon in the narrative.
- John 4:46–54: the royal official's son lies sick at Capernaum. Jesus is in Cana. The boy is healed at a distance. Capernaum is where the child is, not where Jesus is standing.

**Compressed onto this Season.** After Mark 1:14 and Matthew 4:12, Capernaum becomes his home. This is the largest body of episodes on the sheet.

- He leaves Nazareth and lives in Capernaum, by the sea, in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali (Matthew 4:13). Matthew 4:14–16 quotes Isaiah 9:1–2 over that move. Mark 2:1 calls the house there "home."
- In the synagogue, an unclean spirit (Mark 1:21–28; Luke 4:31–37). They have come in from the lake. The call of the fishermen is filed under the Sea.
- Peter's mother-in-law, and the crowd at the door after sunset (Mark 1:29–34; Matthew 8:14–17; Luke 4:38–41).
- The paralytic lowered through the roof (Mark 2:1–12; Luke 5:17–26; Matthew 9:1–8, "his own city").
- Levi at the tax booth by the sea (Mark 2:13–17; Matthew 9:9–13; Luke 5:27–32).
- The centurion's servant (Matthew 8:5–13; Luke 7:1–10). This is not the royal official of John 4. See Cana.
- A ruler of the synagogue, Jairus, and the woman who touches his garment (Mark 5:21–43; Matthew 9:18–26; Luke 8:40–56). Mark 5:21 says the crowd is by the sea after the crossing, and it does not print the name Capernaum. It is read as this town because Matthew 9:1 calls the near side "his own city," which Matthew 4:13 has identified as Capernaum.
- After the five thousand, the crowd finds him at Capernaum, and he teaches in the synagogue there (John 6:24–59). John 6:4 says the Passover was near. In Mark, John's death has already been told before that feeding (Mark 6:14–29). This discourse is compression, near the far end of the years 27–29, and it is not the Aenon week.
- The temple tax, and the coin in the fish (Matthew 17:24–27).
- On the way into Capernaum, the argument about who is greatest (Mark 9:33–37). These last two are late in the Galilean ministry, still before the cross, and well after John's death (Mark 9:9–13).
- The woe against Capernaum (Matthew 11:23–24; Luke 10:15). Matthew 11 opens from John in prison (Matthew 11:2). The same saying names Chorazin and Bethsaida. Chorazin is not a label on this sheet.

**Memory in the landscape.** Isaiah 9:1–2 is the passage Matthew applies to the move. It is not a story that happened in the village. Capernaum is not an Old Testament place-name. The prophet Nahum is from Elkosh (Nahum 1:1), and Elkosh is not identified with this town here.

### Bethsaida

John 1:44: Philip, Andrew, and Peter are from Bethsaida. John 12:21 says "Bethsaida in Galilee," in a scene in Jerusalem during the last week, so that verse's scene is off this map and off this Season. The hometown fact is already stated in John 1:44. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Bethsaida, calls it the city of Andrew, Peter, and Philip, in Galilee, on the lake of Gennesaret.

Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.28: Philip raised the village Bethsaida, on the lake of Gennesaret, to a city, and named it Julias. Philip's tetrarchy was east and north of the lake, not Antipas's Galilee. The Gospels never say Julias. They say Bethsaida, and John 12:21 says Bethsaida in Galilee. The tension is real: a Galilean Bethsaida on the west, or Philip's Julias on the northeast shore, with "Galilee" used loosely for the lake. The map pins Bethsaida at the north shore, east of the Jordan's inlet, with Gamla inland beyond it. That is the Julias side. Which ruin is the city, the mound set back from the water or a site on the shore, is debated, and the ancient texts do not decide the ruin. They require a Bethsaida on the lake.

**The text places this here in this Season.** The hometown of Philip, Andrew, and Peter (John 1:44), stated while the first disciples are being called at the Jordan. No scene is played in the town yet.

**Compressed onto this Season.**

- Luke 9:10–17 associates the feeding of the five thousand with Bethsaida. The usual text says he withdrew to a city called Bethsaida. Other copies say a deserted place belonging to the city called Bethsaida. Mark 6:45, after the feeding, sends the disciples by boat toward Bethsaida, which is hard to reconcile with their already being there. John 6:1 puts the feeding on the other side of the sea, and John 6:16–17 sends the boat to Capernaum. The feeding is a shore episode, filed in full under the Sea. Luke is the Gospel that ties it to Bethsaida. The tie is kept, and it is marked as disputed.
- Mark 6:45: the boat is sent ahead toward Bethsaida, into the storm. They land at Gennesaret (Mark 6:53).
- Mark 8:22–26: a blind man is healed at Bethsaida. This is one of the few episodes the text actually sets inside the town. It comes after the four thousand and the landing at Dalmanutha, late in the ministry.
- The woe against Bethsaida, paired with Chorazin (Matthew 11:21; Luke 10:13). Chorazin is not on this sheet.

**Memory in the landscape.** None in the Bible before the Gospels.

### Gamla

Gamla is not in the New Testament. Josephus, *Jewish War* 4.2, describes it as the city opposite Taricheae on the other side of the lake, in lower Gaulanitis, on a ridge shaped like a camel. The map's dot, inland northeast of the sea, matches that.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The Gospels are silent.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** At the census under Quirinius, about 6 AD, Josephus names Judas a Gaulanite, from a city named Gamala, who with Saddok raised a revolt (*Antiquities* 18.4). *Antiquities* 18.23 and *Jewish War* 2.118 call him Judas the Galilean and, in the War sentence, do not name the city. Acts 5:37 also says Judas the Galilean, in the days of the census. Gamaliel's speech is in Jerusalem, after the cross, so the speech is off this map and off this Season. The revolt of 6 AD is memory, and the link from Judas to this city is Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.4, not Acts.

**Outside the Season.** The siege of Gamla in 67 is *Jewish War* 4.1–83. It is not compressed onto this Season.

### Hippus

Hippus is not in the Bible. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Decapolis, lists it with Pella and Gadara, and under Aphek he mentions Hippos as a city of Palestine. Josephus, *Jewish War* 2.97, says that with Gadara it was added to Syria after Herod's death. *Life* 349 puts it thirty furlongs from Tiberias, across the lake. The map's dot, on the heights east of the sea and opposite Tiberias, matches that.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing is assigned to this dot. The demoniac's country is debated, and one geographical argument points at the steep shore below Hippus. The manuscripts say Gadarenes, Gerasenes, or Gergesenes. They do not say Hippus. The episode stays with Gadara, with the debate stated there.

**Memory in the landscape.** None in the Bible.

**Outside the Season.** Justus of Tiberias burns villages of Hippos and Gadara at the start of the war (*Life* 42). That stays off the Season.

## Wilderness

### The Sea of Galilee

Three names in the Gospels: the Sea of Galilee (Matthew 4:18; Mark 1:16; John 6:1), the Sea of Tiberias (John 6:1; 21:1), and the lake of Gennesaret (Luke 5:1). Josephus uses the lake of Gennesaret (*Jewish War* 3.506ff; *Antiquities* 18.28; *Life* 349). The water on the sheet is that lake.

**The text places this here in this Season.** No boat story yet. The early narrative touches the shore at Capernaum (John 2:12) and puts a sick child there (John 4:46). It does not tell a miracle on the water while John is still at Aenon.

**Compressed onto this Season.** The whole Galilean lake ministry, after John's arrest (Mark 1:14), and before the cross.

- The call of Simon and Andrew, then James and John, from their boats (Mark 1:16–20; Matthew 4:18–22). They go on into Capernaum (Mark 1:21).
- The great catch, and the call told with it (Luke 5:1–11). Luke names the lake of Gennesaret. This is a different telling of the call, not the catch after the resurrection.
- Teaching the crowd from the boat (Mark 4:1–2; Matthew 13:1–2; Luke 5:3).
- The stilling of the storm (Mark 4:35–41; Matthew 8:23–27; Luke 8:22–25).
- The crossing to the country of the Gerasenes or Gadarenes, and the return (Mark 5:1–21 and the parallels). The place-name debate is under Gadara.
- The feeding of the five thousand on the shore, then the storm and the walk on the water (Mark 6:32–52; Matthew 14:13–33; John 6:1–21). Luke 9:10–17 tells the feeding and not the walk, and he ties the feeding to Bethsaida. John 6:1 says the other side of the Sea of Galilee, that is, the Sea of Tiberias. John 6:16–21, the boat is going to Capernaum. John 6:23, boats from Tiberias come to the place of the meal. Mark 6:45 sends the disciples toward Bethsaida, and Mark 6:53 lands them at Gennesaret. The shore of the five thousand is disputed between Bethsaida (Luke), a lonely place on the other side (John, and Mark's departure toward Bethsaida afterward), and the west shore where they land. It is kept as a lake episode, with Bethsaida's claim marked under that town. By Mark's order, John the Baptist is already dead (Mark 6:14–29).
- Peter on the water is Matthew's addition to that night (Matthew 14:28–31).
- The feeding of the four thousand (Mark 8:1–10; Matthew 15:32–39). Mark's preceding context is the Decapolis (Mark 7:31), so this shore may be the eastern one. They then cross to Dalmanutha (Mark 8:10) or Magadan (Matthew 15:39). See Magdala.

**Memory in the landscape.** Isaiah 9:1, "the way of the sea," is the line Matthew uses for the move to Capernaum (Matthew 4:15). It is not a lake episode of its own.

**Outside the Season.** The catch of fish and the breakfast on the shore in John 21 are after the resurrection. John 21:1 calls the water the Sea of Tiberias. That chapter is not placed in this Season.

### The Jordan

The river on this sheet is the stretch from the lake south past Hippus, Gadara, Scythopolis, Pella, Salim, and Aenon.

**The text places this here in this Season.** John's baptizing, with a split in the river.

- People are baptized in the Jordan, and Jesus is baptized in the Jordan (Mark 1:4–11; Matthew 3:5–6, 13–17; Luke 3:3, 21–22).
- John 1:28 places John's work at that time at Bethany beyond the Jordan. That site is the lower Jordan, in the region of Jericho, and it is off this sheet. The calling of Andrew, Peter, Philip, and Nathanael (John 1:35–51) belongs there, not at the Aenon pin.
- John 3:23 then places John, still free, at Aenon near Salim, where there was much water. The verse does not say "Jordan." Eusebius places that water near the Jordan, eight miles south of Scythopolis. This stretch of the river carries Aenon. It does not carry the baptism of Jesus.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing further on this stretch.

**Memory in the landscape.** The famous Jordan crossings are downstream, off this sheet: Israel opposite Jericho (Joshua 3–4), and Elijah and Elisha from Jericho (2 Kings 2:4–14). Naaman washes in the Jordan (2 Kings 5:10–14) with no ford named, so he is not pinned to Aenon. Judges 7:24–25, Gideon calls Ephraim to take the waters of the Jordan against the Midianites, in the same campaign as Moreh and the Jezreel. The ford (Beth-barah) is unnamed on this sheet and uncertain. It is not turned into a labeled site.

**Outside the Season.** John 10:40, the return to the first baptizing place across the Jordan, is late and off this sheet. See Aenon.

### The Kishon

The Kishon is not in the New Testament. On the sheet it runs through the western Jezreel, past Legio, toward the sea.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.**

- Judges 4:7, 13 and 5:21: Sisera's army is swept away at the Kishon. Barak comes down from Mount Tabor (Judges 4:6, 12, 14). The same fight is "at Taanach by the waters of Megiddo" (Judges 5:19), which is the Legio end of this river.
- 1 Kings 18:40: after Carmel, Elijah has the prophets of Baal killed at the Kishon. Carmel is off the west edge of this sheet. The river labeled here is that Kishon.

### The Jezreel

The valley runs east from Legio between Moreh and Gilboa to Scythopolis, and the Jordan plain continues south. The map prints **Esdraelon** on the valley floor between Mount Moreh and Mount Gilboa. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Jezreel, knows a great village Esdraela in the great plain between Scythopolis and Legio. Esdraelon is the Greek name of Jezreel. The dot is the town of Jezreel, inside the valley the sheet also labels as the Jezreel. One place, two labels. The town episodes below belong to that dot. The valley episodes belong to the green floor around it.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The Gospels never name Jezreel. Nain overlooks the valley. Nain's episode is filed under Nain.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.** The valley:

- Judges 6:33: Midian, Amalek, and the people of the East camp in the Valley of Jezreel. Gideon meets them from the spring of Harod, with Moreh to the north (Judges 7:1).
- 1 Samuel 29:1: Israel camps by the spring in Jezreel, and the Philistine campaign ends on Gilboa (1 Samuel 31).
- Hosea 1:4–5: Jezreel as the name of Hosea's child, and the bow of Israel broken in the Valley of Jezreel. Hosea 1:11 and 2:22 use the name again. These are prophetic words, not a scene to stage as a battle of this Season.

The town (the Esdraelon dot):

- 1 Kings 18:45–46: Ahab rides to Jezreel, and Elijah runs before him.
- 1 Kings 21: Naboth's vineyard in Jezreel, beside Ahab's palace.
- 2 Kings 9–10: Jehu's coup, Jezebel's death, and the house of Ahab at Jezreel.

### Gennesaret

The plain on the northwest shore, south of Capernaum and north of Magdala, which is where the map prints the name. Mark 6:53 and Matthew 14:34 use it for the landing place. Luke 5:1 uses "the lake of Gennesaret" for the water. Josephus, *Jewish War* 3.516–519, describes the plain's fertility and the fountain the country calls Capernaum, which waters it. See Capernaum for the fountain and the village.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing on the plain while John is at Aenon.

**Compressed onto this Season.**

- Mark 6:53–56 and Matthew 14:34–36: after the walk on the water they land at Gennesaret, and the sick are brought from the whole region. Mark has already told John's death (Mark 6:14–29). This is firmly compression.
- Luke 5:1–11: the great catch on the lake of Gennesaret. The name is the lake's. The boats are the same call filed under the Sea. On the synoptic clock it follows the imprisonment.

**Memory in the landscape.** None under this name in the Hebrew Bible.

### Mount Tabor

Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Tabor, puts the mountain in the great plain, east of Legio. The map agrees: the dome between Nazareth and the lake, north of the Jezreel.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Only by tradition. The Transfiguration is a high mountain, unnamed (Matthew 17:1–9; Mark 9:2–10; Luke 9:28–36). It follows Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi (Mark 8:27), which is off this sheet to the north. Later Christian tradition identified the mountain as Tabor. The main alternative is Hermon, near Caesarea Philippi, also off this sheet. Hermon fits the preceding scene and the height. Tabor fits the traditional pilgrimage site on this map. Placing the Transfiguration on this Tabor is a knowing compression onto a debated mountain.

It is also late. On the way down they speak of Elijah and of John (Mark 9:9–13). John is already dead. This is not the Aenon week.

**Memory in the landscape.**

- Judges 4:6, 12, 14: Barak gathers ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun on Mount Tabor and comes down against Sisera. The battle drains to the Kishon (Judges 5:21).
- Joshua 19:22: Tabor on the border of Issachar. This may be a town at the mountain as well as the mountain.
- 1 Chronicles 6:77: a Levitical town named Tabor in Zebulun. The parallel list in Joshua 21 does not agree name for name, so this is a town name in the text, not a second story about the summit.
- Psalm 89:12, Jeremiah 46:18, and Hosea 5:1 name Tabor in poetry and prophecy. They are not narrative episodes.

**Outside the Season.** In 67 Josephus walled the summit, and Placidus took it (*Jewish War* 4.54–61). He describes a flat top, hard to climb on the north, between the great plain and Scythopolis. That occupation is later than the Season. It also warns against treating the summit as an empty mountain the Gospels have named. The Transfiguration's mountain remains unnamed in the text.

### Mount Gilboa

Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Gilboa, calls it a mountain six miles from Scythopolis, with a village Gelbus. The map puts it south of the Jezreel and southwest of Scythopolis, facing Moreh across the valley. That matches Judges 7 and 1 Samuel 28 together: Saul on Gilboa, the Philistines at Shunem on Moreh's slope, Midian north of the Harod spring by the hill of Moreh.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The Gospels never name Gilboa.

**Compressed onto this Season.** Nothing.

**Memory in the landscape.**

- 1 Samuel 28:4: the Philistines camp at Shunem, and Saul gathers Israel on Gilboa.
- 1 Samuel 31:1–8 and 1 Chronicles 10:1–8: Saul and his sons die on Gilboa. The body is taken to Beth-shan (1 Samuel 31:8–13), which is Scythopolis on this sheet.
- 2 Samuel 1:6, 21: David's lament over the mountains of Gilboa.

### Mount Moreh

Judges 7:1: Gideon camps at the spring of Harod, and the camp of Midian is north of him, in the valley by the hill of Moreh. The map puts Moreh north of the valley, with Nain on its northern slope and the Esdraelon dot to the south. That is the right hill. Eusebius, *Onomasticon*, under Moreh, calls it the name of a hill and does not add a mileage.

This is not the oak of Moreh at Shechem (Genesis 12:6). That Moreh is off this sheet.

**The text places this here in this Season.** Nothing. The mountain is not named in the Gospels.

**Compressed onto this Season.** No episode of the mountain itself. Nain, on its north slope, has the widow's son (Luke 7:11–17), and that episode is compression. It is filed under Nain. The text does not mention the mountain.

**Memory in the landscape.** Gideon's night against Midian, with Moreh as the hill north of his camp (Judges 7:1; the campaign begins in Judges 6:33 in the Valley of Jezreel). Shunem and Endor, on and near this hill, are the neighboring memories named under Nain. They are not labels on the sheet.

## Kept off this sheet

These are easy to drag onto a Galilee map. They stay off.

- Jerusalem, the temple, Nicodemus, and the Passovers (John 2:13–25; John 5; the last week).
- Sychar and the well (John 4:4–42), between Aenon and the second sign at Cana.
- Bethany beyond the Jordan (John 1:28), the baptism, and the first disciples. Also the late return there (John 10:40).
- Caesarea Philippi (Mark 8:27), the confession that precedes the Transfiguration.
- Chorazin, named only in the woe with Bethsaida (Matthew 11:21; Luke 10:13).
- Carmel, the west end of the Kishon story (1 Kings 18).
- Kursi / Gergesa, the third candidate for the demoniac.
- The arrest, the cross, and John 21.
